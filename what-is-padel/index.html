The basics

Tennis rules. Squash walls. Ten minutes to learn.

Padel is the fastest growing sport in the world, and the reason is simple: almost anyone can have a real rally in their first session. Here is everything you need before you walk on court.

How it works

Six things and you're playing.

Always doubles

Four players, two a side. It is a social sport by design — you are never on court on your own.

The walls are in play

The court is enclosed in glass and mesh. Let the ball bounce, then play it off the back wall like squash.

Underarm serve

You serve underarm, below waist height, diagonally into the opposite box. No serve to be intimidated by.

Tennis scoring

15, 30, 40, game. If you have ever watched a set of tennis, you already know how to keep score.

Solid, stringless rackets

Short, perforated and light. Far more forgiving than a tennis racket and much easier to find the middle of.

Smaller court

20m by 10m — about a third smaller than tennis. Less running, more rallies, longer points.

Why people get hooked

It rewards you before you're any good.

Most racket sports make you earn the fun. You spend the first month hitting into the net before a rally lasts long enough to feel like a game. Padel does not work like that.

The court is small, the racket is forgiving and the walls keep the ball alive, so points last. Beginners get long rallies on their first night, and better players still have somewhere to go — the tactical ceiling is genuinely high.

It is also unusually good at mixing levels. A strong player and a first-timer can share a court and both have a game, which is exactly why it spreads through friend groups and workplaces the way it does.

Good to know

  • Low impact on the joints compared with tennis or squash.
  • Ages roughly 8 to 80 all play it, often against each other.
  • Indoors here, so nothing is ever called off for rain.
  • A 90 minute game is as social as it is sporting.

Questions

Before your first session.

Do I need to be fit to play padel?

No. The court is small, the points are quick and you play with three other people, so the workload is shared. Plenty of our members had not played a racket sport in years before starting.

Do I need my own racket?

Not at all. Rackets and balls are available to hire at the desk. Most people hire for their first few sessions before deciding what suits them.

What should I wear?

Normal sportswear and clean, non-marking trainers. Court shoes or trail-style runners give the best grip, but any trainer will do to start.

I have never played. Where do I start?

A beginner group session or a one-to-one lesson. An hour with a coach will get you serving, rallying and using the back wall, which is the part that catches new players out.

How long is a booking?

Courts are booked in 60 or 90 minute slots. Ninety minutes is the sweet spot for four players who want a proper game.

Ready to play

Try it once.

Book a court with three friends, or start with a beginner lesson and let a coach do the explaining. Either way you'll know within an hour.
